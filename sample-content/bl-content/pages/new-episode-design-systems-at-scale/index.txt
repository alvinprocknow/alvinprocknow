<p>This week we're joined by three guests who have each built design systems used by teams of a hundred or more: a component library lead, a design ops manager, and an engineer who spent two years migrating a legacy codebase onto a shared token system. The conversation ended up less about tooling and more about organizational trust &mdash; who gets to change a shared component, and what happens when they're wrong.</p>

<p>One recurring theme: every team we talked to eventually had to decide between a "benevolent dictator" model, where one person or small group owns the system outright, and a "federated" model, where any team can propose changes through a review process. Neither approach is free &mdash; the dictator model moves fast but creates a bottleneck, and the federated model scales better but moves slower and requires much stronger documentation.</p>

<p>We also dug into the unglamorous part of design systems: deprecation. Everyone wants to talk about shipping the new button component. Almost nobody wants to talk about the eighteen-month process of migrating forty screens off the old one. Our guests shared some genuinely useful tactics here, including codemods, usage dashboards, and a deprecation Slack bot that got surprisingly effective results just by being mildly annoying on a schedule.</p>
